The Problem

Austria has significant stocks of vacant, underutilised, and potentially developable land — but finding it requires navigating fragmented cadastral databases, proprietary tools, and opaque administrative processes. The information exists; what’s missing is a way to work with it that is local, sovereign, and built for the communities who need it most.

Rather than building more, the starting question is: what is already here, and how can it be brought back into life?

Unlocking Housing is a suite of geo-mapping modules that runs on your own machine, connects to public Austrian spatial data sources (cadastre, INSPIRE, land register), and produces actionable maps of where housing development may be possible — without sending your research to a third-party server.

Module Suite

The codebase is a SvelteKit monorepo organised into focused, independently usable modules. Each can be run standalone or composed into a full analysis pipeline.

map-core

Map Core

MapLibre GL JS base viewer, tile serving, and shared geo utilities.

geo-layers-at

Austrian Geo Layers

Adapters for Austrian open data: cadastre, planning zones, HORA flood risk, WMS endpoints.

vacancy

Vacancy Detection

Spatial heuristics for identifying potentially vacant or long-term non-used properties.

bioregions

Bioregions

Watershed, soil, and ecological connectivity overlays for bioregional context.

listings-matcher

Listings Matcher

Cross-reference property listings with cadastral data to surface discrepancies and opportunities.

writeback

Logseq Writeback

Export property investigations directly to your Logseq knowledge base.

The Bio Pillar

Unlocking Housing is the flagship project of the evoBIOsys-bio pillar — the branch of EvoBioSys concerned with living, biological, and bioregional systems. Where the Sys pillar builds digital infrastructure and the Evo pillar supports human development, the Bio pillar works with the living substrate: land, housing, bioregions, and the material conditions of settlement.

What evoBIOsys-bio builds toward

The Bio pillar’s long-term work is to develop tools and frameworks for communities to understand, steward, and inhabit their bioregions more intentionally. Unlocking Housing is the first tool in that suite: starting with the question of where can people live? and building toward deeper questions of how should communities relate to the land they occupy?

The bioregional perspective is not decorative. Every analysis in Unlocking Housing includes watershed context, soil data, and ecological connectivity — not because planners usually ask for it, but because these dimensions shape whether a development will be resilient over decades, not just permitted for next year.
